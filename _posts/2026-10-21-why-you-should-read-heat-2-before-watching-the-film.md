I've hung a big-ass, gold-framed print of the diner scene in our living room. My side-quest in life is to reopen [Kate Mantilini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Mantilini). I've watched our mutually-agreed-upon greatest film of all time at least 15 times. 

So you know my word counts, brother. 

![](../images/2026-10-21-why-you-should-read-heat-2-before-watching-the-film/heat-picture-living-room-20261025220915738.jpg)

Us fans have one thing in common: we're going to walk out on everything in 30 seconds flat when we spot Heat 2 at the box office. But when it comes to the book, we're divided into three types:

1. "I'll watch the film first, then (maybe) read the book." 
2. "I'll read the book, then watch the film."
3. "I'm not much of a reader, so I don't care about book." 

Most are the first type -- they want to watch the film first, then read the book, that is, if they ever get around to it. And I get that. On paper it makes perfect sense: Heat is a film. Michael Mann's primary medium is film. So as a fan you're thinking, "When the film comes out, I want to go into the theater fresh, without any prior information, without any spoilers, because I want to experience Heat 2 just as Michael Mann intended. That's why I don't want to read the book first." 

But the reality is, in fact, the opposite. Reading the book, that too before you watch the film, is the only possible way to experience Heat 2 just as Michael Mann wanted to make it. Let me explain. 
## An uncorrupted experience with the original cast
If Michael Mann had a magic wand that could make Pacino, De Niro, Kilmer (RIP) and the others 1995-young again just long enough so he could film Heat 2 with them, I bet he'd wave it faster than you could say 'Slick.' I bet he'd give anything to film Heat 2 with the original cast. So would you. So would I. Fuck, I'd let Albert waste my motherfuckin' *life*. 

But the thing is, I already did. No, not let Albert waste my motherfuckin' life; watched Heat 2 with the original cast. How? By reading the book. No no no, don't roll your eyes. And don't say, "But Jackie, reading books and watching films are totally different things. How can you compare them?" Normally, I'd tend to agree with you, but not on this one, because this book is rare. We'll get to that in a bit, but my point is, you can have it -- experience Heat 2 with the original cast. All you have to do is read the book. 

But there's a catch: you'll need to read the book *before* you watch the film. If you try to read it after, you'll have the faces of the new cast interfering in your mind as you imagine the characters and events of the book.

That's why I think reading the book before before watching the film is the only possible way to experience Heat 2 like it was meant to be: with the same Neil, Hanna, Vincent and others in their uncorrupted glory, without anyone else taking their place. That's how I experienced it, and it was pure and special. 

Now let's come back to why this book is rare and what actually makes it feel just like you're watching a film.
## A real novel, not a pitch novel 
I'll be able to better convey to you why this book is special if I establish some context first, so please bear with me for a couple of paragraphs as I tell you about another experience I had. 

A few months ago I read a few pages from the book version of 'Once Upon a Time in Hollywood'. Its cover said it was a novel, but I noticed something weird. The paragraphs followed a pattern that went like this: 

He/she said, "*some dialogue*".  He/she did *some action*. *Something* happened. 

Novels don't usually read like that. I realized it was actually the film screenplay (or script) published as-is, without any efforts put in to adapt it to the crime novel format. So I didn't bother reading further.

The reverse happens too. Sometimes authors write "novels" with the sole intention of turning them into films later, so they write them more like screenplays and less like novels, hoping to catch the attention studio execs. The problem is, novels and screenplays are fundamentally at odds with each other in how they tell stories. Internal monologue is the bread and butter of a novel. But the number one rule of cinema is show, don't tell. So these authors (over-)rely on spoken dialogue and detailed physical action to tell the story, completely forgetting about internal monologue, and the reader is never able to get into the heads of the characters, feel what they're feeling, connect with their emotions, connect with their reality, truly experience it. Due to lack of novelistic craft, the reading experience of such fake "novels" is always subpar. I've unfortunately read a couple like these and I hate them. 

Heat 2 is not like these fake novels. Our dear Michael Mann has gone the extra mile and collaborated with a real crime fiction author to write Heat 2 like it was a real crime novel, not just some Hollywood "pitch novel" meant to attract producers. But it's not just a crime novel either. It combines novelistic craft with the detailed action of a movie screenplay. I was able to experience it as both book and film and it was a treat for the senses. You don't usually find such experiences. I could create the set in my head, watch the action, play the scenes (with the original cast, by the way) like it were a film, *and* get into the heads of Neil, Hanna and Chris, feel what they were feeling, have the satisfaction of having read a good crime novel. It was beautiful. This is what you get when a director and a novelist combine their crafts. Michael Mann and Meg Gardiner have done justice to the crime novel format and the reader. 

 Initially I'd been scared to pick this book up because you know how it is with much-awaited sequels - they sometimes turn out to be shit. But not this one. I highly recommend you read this book, especially if you love reading.

Let me conclude with one more argument for how reading Heat 2 before you watch it can actually improve your experience of the film.
## Don't judge the film for something it was never meant to do 
The film doesn't deserve to be judged for something we all know it can never do: It cannot hit the same without the original cast. But if you read the book, you will already have enjoyed it with the original cast, so you'll go into the cinema knowing that you shouldn't judge the film for this and enjoy it for what it is, without any subconscious biases towards or longing for the original cast clouding your judgment of the story. Because it is one hell of a good story. 

Don't get me wrong, I'm not saying Heat 2 will not satisfy us. No, I'm fully confident it will hold its own. But something will be missing, no matter what Michael Mann does, simply because it isn't the original cast.

Because the original cast was special. The original Neil, Hanna, and Chris were special. The original cinematography, color palette, and look were special. 1995 was special. The feel of the time was special. And you can have all of that goodness in this story. If you want. Have Heat 2 look and feel just as good as the Heat you love, with the same cast that you love. Have both fuse into one story, one film. Consistent, flowing, like it hasn't been three decades, like it was never really over. One cohesive masterpiece.
